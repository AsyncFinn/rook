# Physics

`rook-physics` and `rook-humanoid`. Part of the [architecture](../architecture.md).

- **Assemblies**: parts connected by rigid joints (`Weld`, `WeldConstraint`, legacy surface joints, and `Motor6D` with its `Transform` applied as a kinematic offset) form one assembly. Each assembly maps to one Rapier rigid body with compound colliders. Each anchored part roots its own assembly, so anchored parts joined to each other stay separate. An anchored part on its own (no joints, no velocity: most of a large map) has a collider and no body, Rapier's (and PhysX's and Jolt's) static geometry, since Rapier's every step costs time for each body, moving or not (84k fixed bodies took 1.4 ms a 240 Hz step), and every collider on a shared body is read again when one on it moves; the rest, and any such part once it gets a joint or a velocity, have fixed bodies of their own. Root-part selection follows Roblox's documented rules. Assemblies are rebuilt incrementally from the journal. A rebuild finds the assemblies of all the parts it touches in one pass, walking each cluster of unanchored parts once (`assembly::Clusters`): a welded map joined to its anchored parts through dozens of joints is one cluster, and walking it once per joint made one changed part cost a whole-map walk per joint. A change to a part's `Material`, `CustomPhysicalProperties` or `CanTouch` doesn't rebuild anything: the part's collider gets its new mass, friction, elasticity and touchability in place, and its body and what touches it wake up, since a resting part reacts at once in Studio. `CanCollide` and `CollisionGroup` still rebuild, because they change which colliders touch and so which touch events fire. The joint and assembly rules read only the DOM (finding a part's joints through its reverse references), so what scripts see happens synchronously: a `CFrame` write moves the whole assembly, and `GetConnectedParts` and `AssemblyRootPart` are always current.
- Non-rigid constraints map to Rapier joints or force controllers. Joints: legacy hinges (Rotate, RotateV, RotateP) and HingeConstraint are revolute joints whose motors are set once per frame from their surface inputs or actuator; BallSocket is a spherical joint; Rope and Rod are distance limits. Force controllers (`movers.rs`): the legacy BodyMovers, RocketPropulsion, SpringConstraint, VectorForce and Torque are read from the DOM when they, their attachments or their parts change, then set each body's forces before every 240 Hz step from its current velocity, so stiff controllers (BodyPosition's defaults) are solved implicitly and stay stable. Rocket `Fire`/`Abort` reach physics as commands queued on the scripting world.
- **Timestep**: the standard fixed-timestep loop. `Physics::step` runs whole 240 Hz steps and carries what's left of the frame to the next, so a simulation doesn't depend on the frame rate; scripts and replication see the stepped state, as measured in Studio. For drawing it keeps the moving bodies' poses from before their last step, and `drawn_poses` interpolates between those and the current ones by the carried time. Each client frame the engine writes them into the DOM's drawn poses (`Dom::set_drawn`), moving the current camera by its subject's offset, and everything that draws a part reads `Dom::drawn_cframe`, never `CFrame`. Only parts drawn somewhere new redraw: an awake body that didn't move (a part resting in an awake island) is drawn at the same pose and costs the renderer nothing. A server draws nothing and keeps none.
- **Server assemblies on a client** (`remote.rs`) are played back a little behind the newest update by snapshot interpolation (docs/behavior/replication.md#physics). Applying a server frame's updates puts the DOM back to where the assemblies are shown, and only for the assemblies those updates moved (`Playback::received`): the rest already show where they are. On a place with 217 played-back assemblies (1,746 parts) and about 113 updated a frame, that halves the write-back.
- **What depends on a part**: constraints and movers are listed on the parts they hold (`joints/attached.rs`), so moving or rebuilding a part re-reads only those, not every constraint in the place. `Physics::generation` counts changes to what queries see (parts added, moved or changed, bodies stepped, terrain built), so a caller can reuse a query's answer while it holds still: the mouse does.
- Shapes: Block → cuboid; Ball → sphere; Cylinder → cylinder along the part's **X** axis; Wedge/CornerWedge → convex hulls; MeshPart and unions → the hulls saved in them, or their box without any.
- `CanCollide`/`CanTouch`/`CanQuery`, collision groups (Rapier solver groups, so groups that don't collide still touch unless `TouchesUseCollisionGroups` is on), and `Touched`/`TouchEnded` from contact events. Friction and elasticity mix by their weights in a Rapier contact hook, as measured in Studio.
- **Queries** (legacy `FindPartOnRay`/`FindPartsInRegion3` and their variants, `Raycast`, `Blockcast`, `Spherecast`, `GetPartBoundsInBox`/`InRadius`, `GetPartsInPart`, `GetTouchingParts`, and the mouse) run on Rapier's query pipeline over the simulation's own colliders, so part shapes are defined once (`rook-physics/src/shape.rs`). Scripts often create or move a part and query in the same frame, so each query first syncs the world with the DOM's journal and puts the colliders of bodies moved since the last step in place, broad phase included; filters (`CanQuery`, ignore lists, groups) are read from the DOM per candidate. The physics world therefore lives in the scripting `World`, next to the DOM. Every DataModel has one: servers step it; a client's is built on its first query and never steps (it answers LocalScripts' and the mouse's queries from replicated positions) until network ownership arrives.
- **Part boxes** (`query/bounds.rs`): every Workspace part's `Size` box in a BVH, which Region3, `GetPartBounds*`, `MakeJoints` and FloorWire floors search, as Roblox finds parts by their extents (the colliders' bounds don't hold a turned cylinder's or wedge's box). It is kept where the colliders are: a part goes in when physics adds it (a batch of a sixteenth of the tree or more rebuilds it in bulk, as at load), is marked when its body or static moves (`Physics::body_moved`, `Physics::static_moved`, and every body a step moved), and is dropped when physics removes it. Marked boxes are read again from the DOM when a box query reads the tree, and `Physics::parts_by_box` asserts physics has taken the DOM's latest changes, so it can't lag the colliders; raycasts never pay for moving parts. As in Rapier's broad phase, leaves carry a skin, so small moves leave the tree alone and larger ones re-insert in O(log n). A search costs what is near it, not the place.
- **Terrain** (`rook-physics/src/shape/terrain.rs`): Workspace's Terrain collides and is queried as the surface `rook-terrain` meshes, one triangle-mesh collider per chunk and material with no body, static geometry like an anchored part's, so building one chunk doesn't place every other again or wake what rests on them (water's a sensor), mapped to the Terrain instance like a part's colliders. A chunk's colliders are built the first time a moving body or a query comes near it (`terrain/near.rs`), so a large map costs nothing at load.
- **Humanoid** is its own system: state machine, floor detection, HipHeight, WalkSpeed, JumpPower/JumpHeight, climbing, seats, health and death, and joint breaking. It drives the root assembly. Every behavior is tagged Approximation until checked against Studio recordings.
- **Resting mechanisms must sleep** (design, not built yet). On a place with 15,800 Workspace children, mostly anchored, the server's `simulate` costs about 4 ms a frame, 3.75 of it Rapier's four 240 Hz steps (solver 1.7, narrow phase 1.0, broad phase 0.25, constraint upkeep 0.37, body update 0.3); `Physics::sync`, Humanoids, movers and terrain together are under 0.2 and `write_back` 0.33 (2026-10-30, throwaway timers). Statics cost nothing per step. Of 345 unanchored assemblies, 237 are awake every step, and 192 of those are three parked trains: each is four cars (about 500 mass) on eight bogies (24) carrying 64 hinged discs (4), resting on anchored rails, that never settle. One such train alone keeps 71 bodies awake for 1.4 ms a frame. Rapier already sleeps by island as PhysX, Jolt and Box2D do, so sleeping isn't missing; the trains never qualify. Two causes, both measured: the solver can't carry a 125:1 mass ratio through two hinges in 4 iterations, so the cars bob at about one step's gravity (0.8 studs/s) and wheels sit 0.002 to 0.007 studs into the rails, past Rapier's 0.005-stud allowance, and get pushed out every step, which trips Rapier's pose-drift sleep rule; and Rook's sleep and contact tolerances are Rapier's meter defaults read as studs, several times tighter than Roblox's ([sleeping](../../behavior/physics/sleeping.md)). Not the cause: restitution on remade contacts, the discs' cylinder shape (boxes bob the same), wheel-wheel contacts, joint warm-starting. Raising the iterations to 16 brings the train to 0.03 studs/s with 69 of 71 bodies ready to sleep (two free-spinning discs keep it awake), at twice the cost while awake. The fix follows what established engines do for jointed mechanisms: solve a hinged tree in reduced coordinates (PhysX articulations, Bullet's `btMultiBody`, Rapier's multibody joints), so joints hold exactly whatever the mass ratio and only contacts iterate; together with Roblox's tolerances (Rapier's `length_unit` at Roblox's documented 3.57 studs per meter, contact recycling kept at 0.05 studs), a parked vehicle then settles and sleeps as in Studio, and the per-frame cost follows what moves. Rapier's multibody joints place bodies from their joint frames and must form a tree, so a hinged assembly graph with loops keeps impulse joints for the closing edges.
- **Contact load**: Rapier 0.36 already merges a pair's manifolds that share a contact plane, solving at most 4 points per plane (`contact_clustering`, on by default in 3D), and reuses a pair's contacts while its relative pose barely moves (`contact_recycling`, which Rook keeps working by asking for its contact hook only where mixing needs it, `contacts::hooks_for`). So a union pair's dozens of per-hull manifolds don't reach the solver one by one, and resting pairs skip the narrow phase. On a place with moving machinery built of unions (about 85,700 colliders, 113 awake server bodies), the narrow phase still costs about 2.4 ms a frame because those pairs move every step (2026-11-03, Rapier's `profiler` counters).
- **Adaptive timestepping** (deferred). Roblox's default steps each moving assembly at 240, 120 or 60 Hz by its speed against its size, finding contacts only at its own steps (measured: [timestepping](../../behavior/physics/timestepping.md)); Rook steps all of them at 240, which is Roblox's `PhysicsSteppingMethod.Fixed`. Rapier can step parts of one world at different rates: each body asks for extra solver substeps, and each connected group takes the most any of its bodies asks for. That alone isn't Adaptive, since Rapier finds contacts once per world step and Rook's own per-step work (movers, the humanoid controller, the falling-contact hook) runs between world steps, not substeps. Matching Roblox needs contacts found at each assembly's own rate and that work solved inside the step as constraints.
- Rapier is the foundation, not the specification. Where Roblox's solver differs (friction combine, elasticity, joint stiffness, sleeping), Rook's semantics layer adjusts, and each difference gets a provenance note.
