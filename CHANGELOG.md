# Changelog

## Versioning

Rook's version says how far along the roadmap it is:

- Every change goes under **[Unreleased]** as it lands, without a version of its own.
- **Minor** (`0.N.0`): finishing milestone N releases what's collected. 0.3.0 finished M3 Physics, 0.4.0 M4 Content and 0.5.0 M5 Audio. Version 1.0 comes with M22, the release; after it a minor comes with each finished set of new features.
- **Patch** (`0.N.y`): fixes collected under [Unreleased] are released together, at most once a week. Single fixes, roadmap steps and pushes don't get a version of their own.
- Commits go to GitHub as they land, so [Unreleased] on GitHub shows what the next version will hold.
- Before 1.0, any release may break things.

Versions were renumbered twice: on 2026-10-15 (every step had been a minor version, 0.4.0 to 0.13.1) and on 2026-10-16 (every fix had been a patch of its own, up to 0.5.17), when commit messages were rewritten to match.

Every crate and app shares the workspace version. Each release is tagged `vX.Y.Z` in git. The pinned Roblox version (currently 0.741.19) is separate and listed under each release.

## [Unreleased]

- Highlights draw as in Studio. A Highlight fills the parts it covers (its `Adornee`, or its parent part or Model) in exactly `FillColor` at `FillTransparency`, mixed into the finished picture, and outlines them in `OutlineColor` two pixels wide, around a Model's parts as one shape; `DepthMode` AlwaysOnTop shows both through walls and Occluded only where the parts are seen. One with an `Adornee` works wherever it is, as in Studio. A same-scene comparison with Studio puts the outlines on the same pixels.
- Line, Image and Wireframe HandleAdornments and SelectionPartLassos draw as in Studio. A LineHandleAdornment runs `Length` along its LookVector, a few pixels across whatever its distance (0.35 pixels per `Thickness`, as Studio draws it). An ImageHandleAdornment shows its `Image` tinted by `Color3` on a square of `Size` turned with its `CFrame`, seen from in front. A WireframeHandleAdornment draws the one-pixel lines scripts give it with `AddLine`, `AddLines` and `AddPath`, which `Clear` empties, from any script. A SelectionPartLasso, though deprecated, still draws in Studio: a quarter-stud bar from its Humanoid's root part to its `Part`, following either as it moves. M7 step 6 is done.
- FloorWire textures draw as in Studio: its `Texture` shows every `StudsBetweenTextures` along the wire, counted back from `To`, as two crossed images `TextureSize` big that stand on their side on the rising and falling legs, moving towards `To` at `Velocity` studs a second from `CycleOffset`. The wire also keeps 0.2 studs off the floor and out from its parts' faces as Studio's does, and follows `To` when only `To` moves.
- Reflections look like Studio's. Parts reflect the sky itself, read from a cube of it blurred by each material's roughness as Studio's shaders blur it, and weighted by Studio's own BRDF table from your install, where they used to reflect the skybox's average colour. Reflectance mirrors the sky sharply, clouds and all, and the mirror is lit like any surface, as in Studio; it only works on Plastic and SmoothPlastic parts and untextured meshes, as in Studio, so Reflectance on Metal, Wood, Neon or a textured mesh no longer tints them. Metals keep ambient and local light on their colour, as Studio's shaders do. On a row of reflective blocks and a mirror ball under the default sky, Rook is now 7.8 levels from Studio on average (47.7 before).
- Handles, ArcHandles and FloorWires draw as in Studio. Handles show a ball off each of their `Faces` (Style Resize) or an arrow out of it (Movement), and ArcHandles a red, green and blue ring around each of their `Axes` with two grip balls; the grips keep one size on screen, as Studio's do. A FloorWire is laid from its `From` part down to the floor under it, along X, then Z, and up to its `To` part, with balls bulging at the corners. Dragging handles comes with GUI input.
- SelectionSpheres, SurfaceSelections and the Box, Sphere, Cylinder and Cone HandleAdornments draw as in Studio. A SelectionSphere is a ring facing the camera, about a stud wide, sized by its part's smallest side, in exactly its `Color3`, with `SurfaceColor3` filling its hole. A SurfaceSelection outlines its `TargetSurface` with 0.4-stud bars on the face's edges. A HandleAdornment's shape sits at its `CFrame` and `SizeRelativeOffset` from its part (cylinders along Z with `InnerRadius`, cones pointing along the LookVector); `Shading` Default draws it without light, Shaded lit, XRay and XRayShaded show it at half strength through parts in front, and `AlwaysOnTop` draws it over everything. Same-scene comparisons with Studio land on the same pixels, within a level or two of its colours. Every adornment kind now redraws its part when it changes. Not yet: Line, Image and Wireframe HandleAdornments.
- SelectionBoxes draw as in Studio: on their `Adornee` (not on a part they merely sit in), each edge a bar twice `LineThickness` across centred on the part's edge, in `Color3` without light, with `SurfaceColor3` filling the box at `SurfaceTransparency`; `Transparency` and `Visible` work, and parts in front hide them. Moving one to another Adornee redraws both parts. A same-scene comparison with Studio lands on the same pixels and colours.
- Explosions go off as in Studio. One entering Workspace hits every part its BlastRadius reaches (`Hit` gets each with its distance), breaks the Welds, Snaps, Glues, Motors and Motor6Ds within DestroyJointRadiusPercent of it (WeldConstraints and hinges stay), and pushes every unanchored part straight away from it by BlastPressure × the square of its size, spinning it as Studio does: a 1-stud cube leaves at 117.5 studs/s at the default pressure, the speed measured in Studio. Craters carve Terrain as `FillBall` with Air does. Parts under a ForceField aren't broken off or pushed. The server removes it 2.75 seconds later. Its look is Studio's in five layers from the install's images: a white flash, a fireball turning from yellow-white to brown, dark smoke rising, a spray of specks and a faint ring, the same size whatever its radius; `Visible` and `TimeScale` work. Particles now stay with their own effect when others come and go. Not yet: Explosions a LocalScript makes do nothing but draw, and killing characters waits for Humanoid health.
- A server DataModel has a `NetworkServer` under it and a client a `NetworkClient`, as in a Studio playtest, so scripts that check `game:FindFirstChild("NetworkServer")` to tell where they run work: an old analytics module an archived place loads refused to start without it. A second `require` of a module whose body is still running on another thread now waits for it and gets the same value, as in Studio, instead of raising "required recursively".
- Terrain water looks like Studio's. It reflects the place's skybox, where it used to reflect a two-colour gradient. Looking down it reflects about a quarter of the sky, rising to nearly all of it seen along the surface, so a low camera sees a pale, sky-coloured top that meets the teal sides in a crisp line, as in Studio. Its body is lit as Studio lights it, a little brighter than a part and the same on every face, and only a fifth of WaterTransparency of what's behind it shows through (it was all of it, so sand under default water showed plainly). It's still drawn at WaterTransparency 1, as in Studio. Its sides ripple like its top and round over the edge without a seam. The sun glints off small ripples, on the sun's side only. From straight above, a pool's colours at four depths and five settings are within 6 levels of Studio's. Like Studio's at its default quality, it reflects the sky but not parts (Studio adds screen-space reflections of parts only from quality level 16 up).
- Big places spend less of each frame on the CPU. Parts that only moved keep their slots and get only their new place written, instead of being looked up and placed again. Light buffers are only rebuilt when a light changes, and the parts blocking light are only re-read while something uses them (the sky's light, or a light with `Shadows`). Transparent parts keep their instances and are sorted again only when they or the view change. Batches are culled 32 at a time before any one is looked at, on every core, for the camera, the sun and image layers at once; scattered instance changes go up in a few big writes. Particles out of view are skipped one by one, each emitter's are built on a core of their own, and they're sorted as small keys. The player uses mimalloc instead of the system allocator, which on Windows took a fraction of a millisecond for each block of tens of kilobytes once a big place had filled its heap; that slowed every part of the frame, scripts and physics included. On a 104k-entry place with 1,644 moving parts and 319 emitters (offscreen at 1920 × 1080, medians), the CPU's recording of a frame went from 14.8–17.7 ms to 4.5–4.7 ms, the scene update from 17–22 ms to 7.5 ms a frame on average (median 3.3–3.7 ms), and the whole frame from 77 ms to 38–42 ms. `--bench` prints the scene update's median and where the CPU's recording of a frame goes, and `--turn <degrees>` turns its camera each frame, as someone looking around does.

- ParticleEmitters, Beams and Trails draw. A ParticleEmitter's particles are born in its part's Shape (Box, Sphere, Cylinder or Disc, in it or on it, going out or in, as Studio sends them) or at its Attachment, at its Rate and Speed, turned by SpreadAngle, with Acceleration, Drag and LockedToPart; their Size, Transparency (envelopes included), Squash and Color change over their lives; flipbook textures play in all four modes; the four Orientations, Rotation, RotSpeed, ZOffset, Brightness, LightEmission and LightInfluence work. A Beam is its Bézier curve between its Attachments in Segments pieces, Width0 to Width1 across their up (or facing the camera), its image repeating and scrolling by TextureMode, TextureLength and TextureSpeed. A Trail lays its pieces as its Attachments move, by MinLength, MaxLength and Lifetime, with WidthScale, Color and Transparency over their lives and Stretch, Wrap or Static images. `ParticleEmitter:Emit` and `Clear`, `Trail:Clear` and `Beam:SetTextureOffset` work from any script, and a server script's `Emit` shows on the player's client. Not yet: VelocityInheritance, WindAffectsDrag and blending between flipbook frames.
- The sun, its highlight and fog are measured in Studio's light units and drawn as Studio's shaders draw them, so sunlit faces match Studio's: the sun gives 0.5 light units per Brightness on a face square to it, turning yellow and dim in the last few degrees above the horizon, and the moon lights at night; surface colours are squared as Studio squares them; the highlight is Studio's own (SmoothPlastic roughness 0.4, Plastic 0.65); fog keeps 2^(1 − t) − 1 of a surface across FogStart to FogEnd and fades unlit particles out. On a rig of white, red, grey, blue and black parts at eight angles to the sun, Rook is now 3.4 levels from Studio on average (14 before; sunlit faces were 10 to 28 levels too bright), and a desert place's sand-coloured Plastic under its own lighting draws (225, 206, 57) to Studio's (227, 210, 58), where it drew (253, 213, 63). Decals on sunlit faces lose an orange fringe Studio doesn't draw.
- Lighting adds up as Studio's does, and goes through Studio's tone curve once, at the end of the frame. Rook draws light in Studio's light units (Ambient's colour squared, Neon's glow) into a frame holding Studio's encoding of them, blends Decals, transparent parts and particles there, and then applies the place's curve: the default one, or Retro's (no curve) when the place's ColorGradingEffect asks for it. Studio's shader files show the frame's encoding. See-through black over Neon now darkens it as in Studio, within 6 levels of Studio's measurements at every Transparency (it used to blend after the curve: a poster Decal's dark areas drew 40 where Studio draws 12). Neon glows at Studio's strength under each curve (grey 128 draws 215 under the default curve, 237 under Retro), and coloured ambient light is as saturated as Studio's (Ambient (171, 60, 0) with OutdoorAmbient (0, 127, 127) lights white as (175, 134, 134); Studio (175, 131, 130)).
- The player's window opens as soon as it starts and shows a dark loading screen (its title says `Loading...`) while the place loads, as Roblox's does, instead of opening only once the place had loaded and showing white until the first frame. The server loads on its thread while the window keeps drawing; Escape or closing the window during loading quits. Text and a progress spinner wait for the GUI milestone.
- Materials draw as sharp as in Studio: their textures are kept at their full 1024 × 1024, as Studio draws them, where Rook used to halve them to 512, softening every material's colour, normal and roughness. They stay block-compressed on the GPU (BC1 for colour and normals, as Roblox serves them, BC7 for the roughness-metalness-height map), so they take less memory than before: ten legacy materials went from 38.7 MB at 512 to 23.0 MB at 1024, and a place's load time and frame time didn't change measurably (0.4 to 0.8 s and 1.8 ms a frame, before and after). Terrain's textures too. A device without block compression gets them decoded at 512, as before. `--bench` prints the GPU memory images hold.
- Each part's material textures start at a spot of their own, as in Studio, so identical parts side by side no longer show identical patterns. Studio rolls a random start per part each time a place loads (the same part, exported as OBJ before and after reopening its place, started 4 studs apart); Rook picks one per part from its id, which holds still while the part exists. MaterialVariants shift the same way.
- A place with two instances saved under the same `UniqueId` prints Studio's error, `DM contains duplicate Unique ids`, when it loads.
- `CFrame.XVector`, `YVector` and `ZVector` are the rotation's columns, as in Studio (`XVector` is `RightVector`); they returned its rows, so any turned CFrame read back transposed.
- A model without a PrimaryPart has its bounding box (`GetBoundingBox`, `GetModelCFrame`, `GetExtentsSize`) turned to its saved pivot, as in Studio, not to the world axes. Archived scripts that spin a model about `GetModelCFrame()`, like a rotating emergency light, now turn it about the right axis.
- A Decal on the Bottom face of a Cylinder part shows the right way up, as in Studio: the cylinder's Bottom quarter turns its image half a turn from a block's Bottom face, which Rook drew it like.
- Material sizes come from Studio itself. Its OBJ export writes a part's material texture and UVs counted in repeats of it, so exporting one part of each material gives Studio's own sizes, with nothing measured on screen. The legacy set now follows those exports: Glass 4; Wood, DiamondPlate and Metal 5; Concrete, Fabric and Grass 6.67; Pebble, Sand, Ice, Slate, Granite, Foil, CorrodedMetal and Marble 10; Cobblestone 14.81; every other material 8. Earlier today's sizes were measured from Studio's screen and mostly wrong (legacy Concrete drew at 26.67 studs per repeat, with pits far coarser than Studio's). Before that, Rook used sizes fitted by eye (Concrete 8, Slate 16, Granite 24). The 2022 set repeats every 8 studs for every material, as Roblox's texture specifications state (1024 × 1024 on an 8 × 8 stud face) and the 2022 exports confirm. The plain, Organic and Wang patterns stay.
- Material textures on wedge and corner wedge slopes repeat as in Studio: its part shaders size a slope's textures by 0.7 × the two sides it spans, not the slope's true length, so a long, low slope shows longer repeats than before (a 2 × 24 slope 18.2 studs of texture, not 24.1).
- Big places draw less: parts are put in the GPU's batches in spatial order instead of scene order (or, for parts whose mesh or image arrived later, no order at all), so each run of 16 that culling tests sits in a small box. A 120k-part place drew 0.84M triangles a frame instead of 2.9M (GPU main pass 5.3 ms down to 3.1 ms); a 30k-part one 0.92M instead of 1.57M.
- Big places run faster: a 120k-part place with 3,155 sounds went from 32 ms to 23 ms a frame (median, offscreen), a 30k-part one from 23 ms to 20 ms. Each frame walked every sound in the place to move playing ones on (2 ms on server and client alike); it now visits only the sounds that are playing or still loading. Parts that move no longer make the light voxels mark every part in the place again: only the moved parts' voxels change. Transparent parts are culled before they're sorted, not after. Mouse and script rays stop at the nearest part instead of testing every part along the ray. `--bench` prints the mouse's median as well.
- Mouse movement and the wheel each pass the same InputObject to every `InputChanged`, as in Studio, updated in place (with its `Changed` firing) instead of a new one per event. Every mouse move used to leave a dead InputObject behind, hundreds of thousands over an hour of play. Conformance cases can now run on the playtest client with the mouse moved through given points (`--! client mouse x,y ...`).
- The command-line tool is now `rook-tools` (it was `rook`): `rook-tools dump`, `rook-tools content`, `rook-tools corpus-run` and so on. It's a tool for working on places and on Rook itself, so the plain name stays free for the launcher planned in M22. `rook-player` is unchanged.
- Rook says what didn't load, and keeps a log of every run. After loading, `rook-player` prints one summary of the files that failed, grouped by cause, with how many instances and files each hit and the first instance: `578 unions (95 files): Authentication required to access Asset (forbidden) (first: Workspace.…)`. `--verbose` lists every failure with its instance path, content reference and reason, and `--verbose content` (or `terrain`, `render`, ...) shows everything one area logs. Assets Studio reports in its Output (missing or not allowed, and every failed sound) are always printed in red, once per file, as Studio does. Failed meshes, unions and images used to be dropped without a word. Rook's messages go through `tracing`, in the console's Studio-style line format. Decoders keep "a version Rook can't read yet" (`unknown-version`) apart from "damaged". Union collision and sound failures are logged too.
- A data folder, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Rook` (`ROOK_DATA_DIR`; Application Support and XDG folders on macOS and Linux), holds:
  - `settings.json`, read leniently, with comments and trailing commas allowed. It sets the cache size, whether asset delivery is on, the Roblox install and the default log level. Flags beat `ROOK_*` variables, which beat the file.
  - A log file per player run, and per long `rook-tools` command (`corpus-scan`, `corpus-run`, `conformance`), at debug detail and without colours. Old logs are cleaned at startup: Rook keeps the last 30 runs or 14 days, and 100 MB at most.
  - Crash reports: a panic that ends the player or `rook-tools` leaves `logs\crashes\<program>-<time>.txt` with the panic, backtrace, last 200 lines logged, versions, OS and GPU.
  - The REPL prompt's history, kept between runs.
  - Kept content: the materials' and terrain's texture packs and their images, pinned to asset versions and never trimmed. `rook-tools content --pin` refreshes the list.

  The download cache stays in `%TEMP%\Rook\cache`. See [data and logging](docs/design/data-and-logging.md).
- `rook-tools logs` prints the newest log's path (`--open` opens the folder). `rook-tools content <place>` takes a place file directly and lists every content reference in it with its outcome. `rook-tools content`'s `-v` is gone: `--verbose` now means log detail everywhere.

- Materials break up their repeats as Studio does, at Studio's sizes, so big Grass, Slate and Concrete baseplates no longer show stripes. Studio's part shaders lay textures three ways: plainly, Organic (two copies blended, each shifted by an amount a smooth noise picks) and Wang tiles (the image is a 4 × 4 atlas of tiles with matching edges). Rook now does all three, with the formulas and constants read from Studio's shader pack and its own code. Every material of both sets was measured in Studio 0.743 on 64-stud faces. Many sizes were wrong: legacy Concrete repeats every 26.67 studs (Rook drew 8), Slate, Granite, Foil and Ice every 40, CorrodedMetal 20, Salt 13.33; in the 2022 set Granite and Foil every 32, Salt, Fabric and Cobblestone 13.33. Ten legacy materials (Rock, Ground, Mud, Basalt, Snow and the other 2021 ones) and seventeen 2022 ones (Concrete, Slate, Grass, Wood, Sand and more) are Organic, and legacy Brick, WoodPlanks, Marble, Grass and Cobblestone are Wang tiles, as their texture packs say. MaterialVariants with `MaterialPattern` Organic break up too. Terrain turns and shifts each layer's texture at random by Studio's `detiling` and `rotation` (its terrain shader's formula), blending copies across a 4-stud grid, so grass fields lose their 16-stud repeat. Rook's captures from Studio's camera now show the same repeats as Studio's: Concrete's at 348 pixels in both, Slate's at 522, none on Grass, Cobblestone and terrain grass (see [materials](docs/behavior/materials.md) and [terrain](docs/behavior/terrain.md)). Organic and terrain textures are read two and three times: with the screen full of textured plates and grass terrain at 1080p, the GPU's main pass went from 0.23 to 0.35 ms.
- Fix: grassy terrain no longer shows white specks. A voxel whose occupancy byte is 0 still holds a little of its material, but a surface vertex whose cell held only such voxels took Air as its material, and Air has no texture or colour, so it drew as a white blob a few studs across blending into the grass. An archived template's hills had dozens; Studio shows none. Those vertices now take their voxels' material.
- `require(assetId)` and `InsertService:LoadAsset` load published models, as in Studio, where both used to raise an error. The calling thread waits while rook-engine fetches the asset through its content loader and cache, then reads it with rook-formats. `require` loads each id once into a parentless `required_asset_<id>` Model and requires its `MainModule`; requiring it again returns the same value, failures included. `LoadAsset` returns a fresh parentless Model each call. Studio's errors for a missing asset, a missing `MainModule` and a client caller are matched (Studio 0.742). Many modules archived places use now need an account at asset delivery, so they still fail with the download error; an old analytics module an archived place loads with `LoadAsset` now loads and runs (see [content](docs/behavior/content.md)).
- Fire, Smoke and Sparkles draw as in Studio (M7 step 2), with the install's particle images (Rook draws its own without one). Fire is glowing flames in `Color`, as wide as about half its `Size` and rising faster the higher its `Heat`, with a core and trail in `SecondaryColor`; Smoke is big lit puffs in `Color` rising at `RiseVelocity`, as thick as `Opacity`; Sparkles throw stars in `SparkleColor`. All three go along their part's UpVector, as in Studio, so steam nozzles facing down blow down and turned fires burn sideways. Particles stay in the world when their part moves, `Enabled = false` lets the living ones finish, `TimeScale` slows them, and scripts changing an effect or moving its part redraw it. Fitted on frozen and stepped Studio 0.742 captures; on the same scene Rook's light over each effect is within about 25% of Studio's (see [effects](docs/behavior/effects.md)). The renderer's particle system takes ParticleEmitter's model, ready for step 3. No measurable cost on an archive place with 16 effects on.

- Fix: sounds no longer stutter when frames are slow. The player's client steps at most four 1/60 s frames per window frame, so its `TimePosition` falls behind the sound device whenever frames are slow, and the mixer jumped back to it once the two were 0.25 s apart. Now a playing sound keeps the device's clock, as a Roblox client does, and jumps only when it is played again or a script sets `TimePosition`; a sound that reached its end stays silent instead of replaying its last moments while the engine catches up. Checked in Studio 0.743 that a client runs its own clock: an offset a client gave a server's sound held while the server played on, while a server seek, `Play`, `Stop` and a set while stopped all reached the client. Rook already replicated only those; a test now checks that a server stepping nine times slower neither pulls the client's position back nor restarts the sound (see [audio](docs/behavior/audio.md)).
- Players get a character (M9 step 1 begun): on joining, when `Players.CharacterAutoLoads` is on, and on `Player:LoadCharacter()`, the server builds one in Roblox's order (creator-docs Player:LoadCharacter, CharacterAdded, StarterGui, StarterPack): the old character goes (CharacterRemoving), StarterCharacterScripts' contents are copied in, the Backpack and PlayerGui are refilled from StarterPack and StarterGui (which used to happen on joining), `Player.Character` is set and CharacterAdded fires (on the client too), then it's parented to Workspace. Its LocalScripts run on the client and its Scripts on the server. For now the body is a Humanoid and an invisible, anchored HumanoidRootPart nothing collides with or touches, on the first enabled SpawnLocation; rigs, walking and respawning come with the rest of M9. A place whose doors are tweened from a StarterCharacterScripts LocalScript (the server fires a RemoteEvent, then snaps each door to its end once the tween's time is up) showed every door standing still, then jumping; now they slide.
- Fix: a reference set on the server before its target is visible to clients (a script setting `ObjectValue.Value` or `Player.Character` and parenting the target afterwards) reaches clients once the target does, instead of staying nil.
- Fix: moving a model whose saved pivot is very slightly skewed (as places save them) no longer scales it further each time: `PivotTo`, `SetPrimaryPartCFrame` and the other model moves are rigid. A door pivoted to a tween's value every frame went to infinity within half a second. `CFrame:Lerp` also returns an orthonormal rotation from skewed ones.
- Local lights with `Shadows` on are blocked by parts, as in Studio's voxel lighting (finishing M7 step 1). A wall keeps all of a light's pool from behind it, and a post one voxel wide leaves a soft shadow that keeps about half the light. Lights without `Shadows` still shine through walls, as in Studio. Parts block as the sky's light uses them: opaque parts with `CastShadow`, as boxes; `GlobalShadows` doesn't matter. A light isn't shadowed by the part it hangs from, so lamps inside their fixtures still shine. Measured in Studio 0.742 behind a wall and a 4-stud post on either side of a light. Rook's shadow behind the post matches Studio's within a few levels; Studio's shadows also lean to one side, which Rook doesn't reproduce (see [lights](docs/behavior/lights.md#shadows)). No measurable cost on a place with 1,620 shadowed lights.
- Fix: a Script that is disabled and enabled again runs again from the top, as in Roblox, instead of never running a second time. Old places replay an animation this way (a valve that turns a little each frame, then sets its own `Disabled`; each click sets `Disabled = false`), so the animation played once and later clicks only played the sound. Disabling a script now stops it, as in Studio: code running right then carries on until it yields, then its threads, `task.delay` calls and connections never run again. Measured in Studio 0.742 with a script that disables itself and one disabled from outside while waiting.
- Fix: `Tween:Pause()` on a tween still in its `DelayTime` does nothing, as in Studio, where Rook paused it.
- Fix: rook-engine builds without its `repl` feature again.
- Mesh files and images near the camera load first, so a place fills in from around the camera outward instead of in file order. The loader's fetch and decode threads take the most urgent file next; the player ranks each file by its nearest part waiting for it, and ranks them again whenever the camera moves 16 studs (the sky and terrain textures come first). On a place with 430 files and an empty cache, files within 200 studs of the camera arrived after 1.1 s on average instead of 1.8 s; everything still finishes at about the same time.
- Roofs and walls block the sky's light (`EnvironmentDiffuseScale`), as in Studio, so roofed rooms in places that set it are no longer lit like the open sky. Studio carries the sky's light down through its 4-stud light voxels: under a roof it spreads in from the edges, further the higher the roof (a black roof 60 studs over an 80-stud room still leaves the middle about 15%), while beside a tall wall with open sky above only a few studs are shaded. Rook's light voxels now do the same, filled from the top a layer at a time around the opaque parts that cast shadows, and a fully covered surface keeps the dim grey Studio leaves. Measured in Studio 0.742 under roofs 3 to 60 studs up, beside a 60-stud wall, and with `CastShadow` off (blocks nothing) or Transparency 0.5 (blocks little); Rook lands within about a tenth of Studio's share of the light, with a softer edge under very low roofs. Parts block as their boxes and terrain doesn't block yet (see [materials](docs/behavior/materials.md#the-skys-diffuse-light)).
- `Lighting.EnvironmentDiffuseScale` lights surfaces with the sky, as in Studio. In places that set it (1 is the default for new places), faces turned away from the sun take on the sky's colour instead of staying dark grey: a white face away from the sun under the default sky drew (151, 199, 219) in Studio and dark grey in Rook, and now (150, 201, 221). The light comes from the skybox's Up, side and Down images, weighted by which way a face points, and adds to Ambient before Studio's tone curve, scaled linearly by the property; it fades at night. Measured in Studio 0.742 at 0, 0.5 and 1 under the default sky, a flat grey Sky and a red, blue and black one. Places saved without the property read 0, so archived places are unchanged. Roofs and walls block it (next entry; see [materials](docs/behavior/materials.md#the-skys-diffuse-light)).
- Fix: see-through Glass filters what's behind it as Studio does, instead of darkening it nearly to black. Rook multiplied by the full Glass colour up to Transparency 0.65 (fitted on red Glass alone), so grey Glass at 0.6 hid what was behind it. Now the filter is the colour raised to a power that falls with Transparency, per channel: black Glass at 0.6 passes 2%, grey 99 about 21%, white all of it. Measured in Studio 0.742 from 45 studs on six greys at seven Transparencies over a wall of greys; from 0.5 to 0.95 Rook lands within 3 to 4 levels (rms). (Seen from hundreds of studs Studio's Glass passes a little more; Rook follows the close view.) - Rook now needs Rust 1.94 or later (for `rbx_mesh`; the code already needed 1.88 for `if let` chains, though 1.85 was declared).
- File meshes (M4 step 3), measured in Studio: SpecialMesh and FileMesh `MeshId`s and MeshParts are drawn, in the part's colour. A file mesh is sized by `Scale` alone and a MeshPart by Size / `InitialSize`, each with the mesh's own origin at the part; mesh children of MeshParts are ignored, those of unions drawn at 1 stud. Files load and decode in the background and appear when they arrive: a new mesh draws nothing until then, and a mesh whose `MeshId` changes keeps its old look until the new file arrives, or for good if it fails. Parts sharing a mesh file share one GPU mesh, drawn instanced. Places without file meshes start no loader.
- `MeshPart.MeshSize` reads the size saved as `InitialSize` (it read 0); `ContentProvider:PreloadAsync` reports `Failure` when a `MeshId` isn't a mesh, as in Studio. Conformance case `content/meshpart`.
- Fixed: a negative mesh `Scale` mirrored the built-in shapes and drew them inside out, and broke the Sphere and Cylinder sizes; Studio ignores the sign for them (observed with a Wedge) and mirrors only mesh files, as Rook now does.
- Permission errors name the capability as Roblox does (`lacking capability NotAccessible`, not `NotAccessibleSecurity`).
- `rook content --place` decodes every mesh reference and totals them by version and failure; `rook corpus-scan` counts mesh instances by kind; `rook-player --fov` sets the field of view.
- Rendering uses reverse Z with an infinitely far plane, so distant surfaces no longer z-fight from depth-buffer imprecision (with the old 0.1–10,000 stud range, depth resolved only to about 0.15 studs at 500 studs away).
- Smoother play: the server runs on its own thread, and simulating and drawing cost much less. Measured on a large 2016 place in normal play (30 s from the start) and on a 2017 place forced through its own round code into the sequence and light sequence (the place's `math.randomseed` fixed so every run plays the same round; t = 60–160 s). A step is one 60 Hz frame of server and client, in lockstep (`rook-player --bench`); window frames are 30 s of live play, uncapped:

  | | step median | step slowest 1% | longest step | window before (vsync / uncapped) | window after, uncapped |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | A large place | 13.5 → 9.9 ms | 39.1 → 34.5 ms | 233 → 249 ms (loading) | 22 / 22 FPS, longest frame 107 ms | 1,590 FPS, longest 7.7 ms, 0 over 16.7 ms |
  | Heavy sequence | 23.2 → 12.0 ms | 59.9 → 36.8 ms | 82 → 93 ms | 23 / 117 FPS, longest 158 ms | 3,010 FPS, longest 4.0 ms, 0 over 16.7 ms |
  | Light sequence | 2.9 → 2.4 ms | 17.9 → 14.1 ms | 29.8 → 23.8 ms | 60 / 688 FPS, longest 24 ms | 3,100 FPS, longest 4.4 ms, 0 over 16.7 ms |

  Output is identical in all three: console logs, a per-10-second digest of every part's position, velocity, material and colour, and a screenshot; so is `rook run-headless` on a large modern place, a small place and a 2008 place. Every conformance case passes. A large modern place's 10 s headless run drops from 33.0 s to 25.9 s; small places run about 0.1 s slower per 10 s (a 2008 place 0.30 → 0.40 s), Rapier's per-step cost for running in parallel.
  - The server steps on its own thread at 60 Hz, so the window waits only on its client and the renderer and draws as often as the display allows. `rook-engine`'s `host::ServerHost` and `ClientHost` only exchange messages; `Session` runs them on one thread. Replication is split into a server `Source`, which describes each change as a self-contained update (current values, new subtrees in full), and a client `Sink`, which maps ids and applies it. `--seconds`, `--bench` and screenshots keep the client in lockstep, so they give the same results every run.
  - `--uncapped` presents without waiting for the display. The window title shows the last second's frame times (FPS, median, slowest 1%, longest, frames over 16.7 ms, server and client steps); `--frame-times` prints them too, with a summary at exit. `--bench` reports server, client and total steps by median, slowest 1% and longest.
  - Collision detection and the solver run on half the cores (Rapier's `parallel` feature, whose results don't depend on the number of threads).
  - The script timeout is checked by a plain C interrupt instead of an mlua closure, which had made every Luau loop iteration three times slower and every call 2.7 times slower. It is the only `unsafe` code in Rook, confined to `rook-lua/src/watchdog.rs`.
  - Reading a plain stored property (`.Parent`, `.Anchored`, `.Name`, ...) and calling `IsA`, `FindFirstChild` or `GetChildren` take a fast path once the class has been seen: the property by a small id, instances returned as keys that the Luau shim turns into userdata. Per call: `.Parent` 0.65 → 0.30 µs, `IsA` 0.82 → 0.48, `FindFirstChild` 0.77 → 0.40, `GetChildren` 2.6 → 1.3. The general path stays the reference for everything unusual.
  - A physics rebuild looks up each part's joints once instead of up to four times, and services are found by class id instead of by comparing names.
  - Drawing: a part that only moved takes its new place from its CFrame; the renderer rewrites just the changed instances and uploads each batch once a frame; transparent parts share one buffer instead of one per part per frame; a part added or removed takes or frees a slot instead of rebuilding the scene (a large place rebuilt all 29,436 parts about once a second, a 45 ms frame).
- Fixed: physics kept bodies, motors and constraints in hash maps with a random order, so a round of light sequence played out differently on every run. It now uses deterministic maps.
- Fixed: holding the right mouse button to look around left the cursor visible and free to wander out of the window. It now hides and stays where it was pressed (held in place, or put back after each move where the platform can only confine it to the window).
- Fixed: changes a server script made while handling a client's message at the end of a frame were trimmed from the journal before the next frame's script and render checks saw them, so a script it parented never started.
- Sun shadows: the renderer draws a 2048² shadow map from the sun (depth only, reverse Z like the main pass) over the opaque parts, fitted around the camera's view out to 300 studs and moved in whole texels so edges don't shimmer. Only the sun's light is shadowed; Ambient and OutdoorAmbient still light shadowed areas. Edges are softened by 3×3 filtered comparison samples. `Lighting.GlobalShadows` off skips the pass entirely, as does a sun below the horizon; `BasePart.CastShadow` off keeps a part from casting while it still receives. Transparent and invisible parts don't cast.
- Fixed: the sun was placed by a textbook solar formula, too low and too far towards +Z (at 14:00 and the default latitude, 33° up instead of 55°). It now follows `Lighting:GetSunDirection()` as measured in Studio: the sun circles the Z axis once a day, rising at +X, on a circle tilted by the latitude less 23.5°.
- Textures and decals (M4 step 4, from Studio measurements):
  - Images load and decode in the background like mesh files (`Want::Image`; PNG, JPEG, BMP and DDS, the formats found among the archive's images), and parts sharing an image share one GPU texture. Uploads are spread over frames, and a frame never waits for decoding or uploading. Places without images pay nothing extra beyond a larger per-part instance.
  - Decals: one image per face (`Face`), oriented as Studio orients it, with `Color3`, `Transparency` and `ZIndex`, on every part shape and mesh. On balls, cylinders and wedges a decal is projected onto the part's box; on file meshes and the Head it goes through the mesh's UVs; on a MeshPart it is projected onto the mesh's box. Decals show on invisible parts, and on half-transparent parts they're drawn whole. Textures tile by `StudsPerTileU`/`V` and `OffsetStudsU`/`V` from each face's top-left corner. Images never cast shadows.
  - Mesh `TextureId` and MeshPart `TextureID`, multiplied by `VertexColor`; version 1.00 meshes' flipped V is confirmed. While a mesh texture loads the mesh is black, and a failed one leaves the part's colour; a decal draws nothing while loading or after failing.
  - Rook's own head mesh has UVs now, measured from the user's `head.mesh`, so face decals sit where Roblox puts them.
  - Plastic draws its surfaces (studs, inlets, universal, and the weld/glue lattice) from the install's `studs.dds`, or Rook's own patterns without an install; SpecialMesh spheres and cylinders show them too. Neon is drawn unlit, and `Reflectance` tints a part towards the sky colour (an approximation). The other materials are plain: their textures aren't in Roblox's install.
- Scripts: setting a Decal's or Texture's `Texture` (or a Decal's `ColorMap`) to a web URL with an `id` stores `rbxassetid://<id>`, and setting `MeshId` or `TextureId` on a SpecialMesh makes it a FileMesh, as in Studio (conformance case `content/decal`). `ContentProvider:PreloadAsync` reports `Failure` for something that isn't an image in an image property.
- `rook corpus-scan` counts materials, surfaces, decal faces, texture tiling, `VertexColor`, `Reflectance` and mesh textures; `rook content --place` decodes every image reference and totals them by format.
- The download cache moved to `%TEMP%\Rook\cache` on Windows, so Storage Sense and cleanup tools can clear it; an existing cache in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Rook\cache` moves there once by itself. Rook keeps the cache under `ROOK_CACHE_MAX` (2 GB by default), dropping what was used least recently, and the new `rook cache` command shows its size or prunes (`--older-than <days>`), trims or clears it.
- Skyboxes: the place's Sky is drawn behind everything, each image placed and turned as Studio does it (Left is at +X and Right at -X, Up's top towards +X), and a place without a Sky shows the default sky from your Roblox install. Reflective parts take on the skybox's average colour, as in Studio. A large 2016 place's barrels (New Yeller, Reflectance 0.3, under an all-black Sky) were washed out towards Rook's default blue; they're olive now, as in Studio.
- Balls and cylinders are built as Roblox builds them (measured from Studio's OBJ export): a box with each face split into a 6 × 6 grid pushed out onto the round surface, each patch mapping images from its box face. Decals on round parts no longer stretch: on a CylinderMesh barrel they were about 13% too wide.
- Fixed: decals on a CylinderMesh were turned 90 degrees (projected in the upright cylinder's axes instead of the part's), and decals fought with other parts' faces lying in the same plane; they now draw on top, as in Roblox.
- Fixed: parts with a BlockMesh drew their studs and inlets; Studio draws them plain (a Brick SpecialMesh keeps them).
- Fixed: with `ROOK_OPEN_CLOUD_API_KEY` set, assets refused anonymously in an earlier run (and cached as refused) weren't retried with the key. The key path is now tested: a large 2016 place resolves 544 of its 704 distinct references with one.
